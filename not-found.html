Hadmatiya હડમતીયા · VS 1951 (1894–95) · status: Unresolved

Named in the VS 1951 travel entry between Sayla and Dharmaj.

Fifteen census villages named Hadmatiya were found across Rajkot, Morbi, Jamnagar, Devbhumi Dwarka, Amreli, Bhavnagar, Junagadh, Gir Somnath, Sabarkantha and Mahisagar districts. None is in Surendranagar or Anand, so the travel route does not disambiguate it. The Tankara (Morbi district) candidate is the most plausible on geography alone, but that is inference, not evidence. Both Wikipedia articles titled Hadmatiya contradict themselves on district. Do not pin this until a Gujarati primary source names the district. OpenStreetMap holds only three nodes of the name in Gujarat — Hadmatiya in Paddhari taluka, Rajkot district (22.43352, 70.48005), which also has a railway station of that name on the Rajkot–Wankaner line; a Hadmatiya at 24.64183, 72.68557 in the north; and a Hadmatia locality at 20.71343, 70.87421. None of the three is attested as His Hadmatiya, and the census count is far higher than three, so this only narrows where to look next. Research pass 1 found no source naming the district; the three OpenStreetMap candidates remain the only ones.

What has already been tried

So that nobody spends the effort twice.

  • OpenStreetMap and Overpass — exhausted. Two Rajchandra-named features exist in the whole of Gujarat, both hospitals. None of the ashrams, temples, houses, stepwells or banyans is mapped, and Nominatim returns nothing for these site names.
  • The trusts' own websites — they publish postal addresses for a handful of sites, all of which are recorded here, and no coordinates anywhere.
  • Google Maps and Places (pass 1) — the avenue that worked. It pinned thirteen sites and located Rajpur. What remains below is what it could not find.
  • Wikimedia Commons — swept for photographs. Eight freely licensed images exist and are now in the map; nothing else of these places is there.

Untried, in the order most likely to pay: Gujarati-language search; the full texts on jainqq.org, particularly the Ardhashatabdi Smarak Granth and the Sachitra Jivan Darshan; yatra accounts on blogs, YouTube and Instagram, where a video walking from a bus stand to a house can identify a building on satellite imagery; Wikimapia and ISRO's Bhuvan; and census and panchayat records for Hadmatiya.

The named Kavitha landmarks - three banyans, a well, a field - are oral and local rather than indexed anywhere, and will not be found by searching maps in any language. They need someone who has walked the village.
