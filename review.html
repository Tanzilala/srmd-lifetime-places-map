Shrimad Rajchandraji — Lifetime Places

Reviewer's sheet · 35 places, 89 sites · 34 places pinned, 1 documented but not located

What this is. Every claim the map makes, with the evidence and the source behind it. It is compiled independently by a devotee and is not an official publication of SRMD, the Shrimad Rajchandra Trust, Raj Saubhag Satsang Mandal or any other body.

What is being asked. Two readings, and they can be done by two different people. The first is the Gujarati reading: are the names, the spellings and the Vikram Samvat dates right? The second is the reading by someone connected to one of the trusts: is the visit real, is the site the right one, and does the building still stand as described?

How to mark it. Tick a box only where you are confident. Where a claim is wrong, strike it and write what is right, and — this matters more than the correction itself — name the source, a book and page or a Trust page. An entry with no source cannot be used, however certain it feels.

Leaving something unmarked is useful. It tells the compiler the claim has not been checked, which is different from it being right.

15. Hadmatiya

હડમતીયા

Taluka and district unresolved, Gujarat · also Hadmatia
not located
VS 1951 (1894–95) · Mumbai & the travel years

Named in the VS 1951 travel entry between Sayla and Dharmaj.

Caution as published. Fifteen census villages named Hadmatiya were found across Rajkot, Morbi, Jamnagar, Devbhumi Dwarka, Amreli, Bhavnagar, Junagadh, Gir Somnath, Sabarkantha and Mahisagar districts. None is in Surendranagar or Anand, so the travel route does not disambiguate it. The Tankara (Morbi district) candidate is the most plausible on geography alone, but that is inference, not evidence. Both Wikipedia articles titled Hadmatiya contradict themselves on district. Do not pin this until a Gujarati primary source names the district. OpenStreetMap holds only three nodes of the name in Gujarat — Hadmatiya in Paddhari taluka, Rajkot district (22.43352, 70.48005), which also has a railway station of that name on the Rajkot–Wankaner line; a Hadmatiya at 24.64183, 72.68557 in the north; and a Hadmatia locality at 20.71343, 70.87421. None of the three is attested as His Hadmatiya, and the census count is far higher than three, so this only narrows where to look next. Research pass 1 found no source naming the district; the three OpenStreetMap candidates remain the only ones.

Chennai (Madras)

Checked without result: SRMD's biography, the Shrimad Rajchandra Trust, English Wikipedia, the Colorado State edition of Life of Shrimad Rajchandra, jainQQ's own Life of Shrimad Rajchandra, the Gujarati Sachitra Jivan Darshan (searched for મદ્રાસ), atmadharma.com, the JAINA biography and rajsaubhag.org. Four further problems: (1) 'Chennai' is a 1996 renaming, so the compiler normalised whatever the original said, exactly as it renders Bombay as Mumbai and Idar as Eedar — the modern form is the compiler's reading, not the source text; (2) it is dropped between Ahmedabad and Viramgam, two towns 60 km apart on one rail line, while every other place in the entry is in Gujarat or Bombay; (3) VS 1955 is the year of renunciation and the three-month Idar retreat, which sits awkwardly with a 1,900 km journey; (4) the 'six days' has no source at all and appears to be borrowed from the VS 1946 entry, 'six days with Ambalalbhai at Sayla.' Kutch
